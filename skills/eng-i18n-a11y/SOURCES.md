# Sources: Accessibility, i18n, time zones and language

Carried over from the research pass of 2026-10-25 (`02-research-i18n-language.md`), product-specific lines removed. Each line keeps the tag it had there: **[V]** checked against the primary page in that pass; **[K]**, **[B]**, **[C]** or **[M]** a known citation (author, year, venue) not re-opened; **[U]** unverified, treat as a hypothesis. Lines without a tag were cited with their venue and date only.

- AssemblyAI documents `expected_languages` as "a hint, not a strict filter" that defaults to `["all"]`. Its `language_confidence_threshold` (0 to 1) makes the request return an error when detection confidence is below the bar. *Verified, AssemblyAI docs, "Automatic language detection", read 2026-10-25.*
- Why detection is unsafe: zero-shot Whisper's language ID scores **64.5%** on Fleurs (Table 5), 13.6 points below the supervised SOTA. Part of the gap is that 20 of Fleurs' 102 languages have no Whisper training data, which caps accuracy at 80.4%; on the 82 overlapping languages the best model reaches 80.3% (§3.6). Radford et al., "Robust Speech Recognition via Large-Scale Weak Supervision", arXiv 2212.04356 (2022); ICML 2023, PMLR 202:28492–28518. *Verified in the arXiv PDF and the PMLR record.* The claim that short or noisy clips do worse is *unverified* (not stated in this paper).
- Language tags follow **BCP 47** (RFC 5646, 2009). Negotiation follows **RFC 4647 (2006)** "Lookup", or **CLDR language matching** (UTS #35 Part 1, "Language Matching"). HTTP `Accept-Language` is RFC 9110 §12.5.4 (2022). *(Verified: RFC 5646 dated September 2009, RFC 4647 September 2006 with Lookup in §3.4, RFC 9110 §12.5.4. The CLDR section name is unverified.)*
- Text language ID is unreliable on short strings. Jauhiainen, Lui, Zampieri, Baldwin and Lindén, "Automatic Language Identification in Texts: A Survey", *JAIR* 65:675–782 (2019), §10.7: "LI of short strings is known to be challenging". They cite Mandl et al. (2006), where all four tested methods had "substantially lower accuracy" on 25-character texts than on 125-character texts. Accuracy also varies widely by method and language count. In the 285-language evaluation the survey cites (Jauhiainen et al. 2017), logLIGA was only slightly over 60% at 70 characters while the original LIGA was almost 85%. Card titles and folder names are exactly this kind of short input. *(Verified in the arXiv v2 PDF. An earlier draft presented the ~60% logLIGA figure as typical, which overstated it.)*
- Microsoft's Globalization guidance on **pseudolocalization** (Microsoft Learn, updated 2022) says: "Resources that haven't been exposed to localization are readily apparent because they won't be pseudo-translated." It advises pseudo-locale names that are well-formed but never real (Windows `qps-ploc`/`qps-plocm`/`qps-ploca`, in the reserved `qaa`–`qtz` range; ICU `en-XA`/`en-XB`). It also gives "lengthen the text by 40%" as a heuristic for English source text. *Verified (page dated 2022-06-07).*
- Externalize strings into a catalog with plural and select support. Unicode **MessageFormat 2.0** became Stable with **CLDR 47 (2025-03-13)** (verified on the CLDR 47 release page: "MessageFormat has advanced from Final Candidate to Stable"). ICU MessageFormat 1 is the incumbent.
- Use property and contract thinking (Claessen and Hughes, "QuickCheck", ICFP 2000): state the invariant once and check it on every tree, not on cases you happen to remember.
- **Nielsen heuristic #9** (NN/g; first published 1994, article updated 2020; verified): "Error messages should be expressed in plain language (no error codes), precisely indicate the problem, and constructively suggest a solution." **#2**: "speak the users' language … rather than internal jargon."
- **RFC 9457 (July 2023, obsoletes RFC 7807)**, verified: consumers "MUST use the 'type' URI … as the problem type's primary identifier". `title` "SHOULD NOT change from occurrence to occurrence of the problem, except for localization", and text language is negotiated with `Accept-Language`.
- **W3C "Working with Time and Timezones"** (I18N WG Group Draft Note, 2025-07-26, verified): use timestamps for events and normalize to UTC. Store the zone when wall time matters, because future wall times depend on zone rules. Identify zones by **IANA IDs**, not offsets. Prefer zone-ID serializations.
- **RFC 9557 (April 2024)**, verified: IXDTF adds a bracketed zone suffix, e.g. `…-08:00[America/Los_Angeles]`, and redefines `Z` to mean the local offset is unknown. **RFC 3339 (2002)** is the base format. The IANA tz database process is **RFC 6557 (2012)**.
- **WCAG 2.2** (W3C Recommendation; current edition 2024-12-12, verified): **SC 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum), AA**: text ≥ **4.5:1**, large text ≥ **3:1**. **SC 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast, AA**: UI components and graphical objects ≥ **3:1** against adjacent colors. Ratio = (L1+0.05)/(L2+0.05) on relative luminance (WCAG definition).
